    The Stribeck curve

    Hersey number = μ N / P  ·  λ = h₀ / √(Ra₁² + Ra₂²)

    Friction in a lubricated contact depends on one group: viscosity times speed over pressure. At low values the surfaces touch (boundary, f ≈ 0.1), in the middle the film carries part of the load (mixed), and beyond it a full film separates the surfaces and friction falls to viscous shear, then rises slowly with speed. The film-thickness ratio λ says which regime you are in: below 1 boundary, 1 to 3 mixed, above 3 full film.

    Journal bearing

    S = (R/c)² μN′/P  ·  h₀ = c(1 − ε)  ·  P = W/(L·D)

    The journal runs eccentric in its clearance; the converging wedge builds pressure that carries the load. A larger Sommerfeld number (faster, more viscous, lighter) means a smaller eccentricity and a thicker film. Heat from shearing the film warms the oil, which thins it, which lowers S: the rig iterates until the film temperature settles. Starve the supply and the film breaks down.

    Rolling bearing life

    L₁₀ = (C/P)^p × 10⁶ rev  ·  p = 3 balls, 10/3 rollers  ·  Lnm = a₁ · aISO · L₁₀

    Double the load and a ball bearing lasts an eighth as long. The equivalent load P blends radial and axial loads with X and Y factors; a deep-groove ball bearing pays heavily for axial load. aISO rewards a good viscosity ratio κ and clean lubricant and punishes contamination, especially at light loads.

    Bearing frequencies

    BPFO = (n/2)·fr(1 − d/D cos α)  ·  BPFI = (n/2)·fr(1 + d/D cos α)  ·  FTF = ½ fr(1 − d/D cos α)

    The geometry sets where a defect will show in a vibration spectrum. The Rotating Machinery Fault Simulator uses these same numbers.

    Friction and heat

    M = f₀ (ν n)^{2/3} dm³ + f₁ P dm  (Palmgren)

    A rolling bearing's friction has a viscous part that grows with speed and viscosity and a load part. Its heat has to leave through the housing; too heavy an oil at high speed is as bad as too light an oil under load.

    About the rig data

    [Illustrative data, for training only.] Journal bearing uses a short-bearing (Ocvirk) load solution with side-leakage flow and a lumped thermal balance; boundary friction 0.10 blended by λ. Rolling bearings use catalogue-style C and C₀ values, simplified X/Y tables, an aISO curve fit and Palmgren friction. Thermal clock speeds the temperature dynamics.

    How to use the rig

    • Journal mode: set load, speed, geometry and oil, press Start. Watch the journal find its eccentricity, the film thickness and λ, the oil warm up and the point move on the Stribeck curve. The speed sweep traces the curve for you.
    • Rolling mode: pick a bearing, set radial and axial load, speed and lubricant. Read the equivalent load, L10 and modified life, friction torque, temperature and defect frequencies; the chart shows life against load.
    • Failure modes: metal contact and wiping, oil overheating, pressure above the babbitt limit, speed above the bearing limit, too little load to keep the elements rolling.
    • Record readings to compare oils, clearances and loads in the tasks.
