    Torque capacity

    T = μ · F · Re · nfaces  ·  uniform wear: Re = (ro + ri)/2  ·  uniform pressure: Re = ⅔ (ro³ − ri³)/(ro² − ri²)

    Hydraulic pressure on the piston makes the clamp force; friction times clamp force times the radius at which it acts is the torque. A new, rigid pad presses uniformly and its friction acts a little further out; once the pad has worn in, pressure is highest at the inner edge (p·r = constant) and the effective radius drops to the mean radius. Design on uniform wear.

    Lining pressure

    uniform wear: pmax = F / (θ ri (ro − ri))

    Every lining material has an allowable pressure. Below it the lining wears predictably; above it the surface glazes, crumbles or fades early. Small pads under high pressure are the usual mistake.

    Energy and temperature rise

    E = ½ J ω²  ·  ΔT = E / (m c)  ·  tstop = J ω / T

    A stop turns all the kinetic energy into heat in the rotor within the stop time. The adiabatic temperature rise is the energy over rotor mass times specific heat; cooling between stops is what stops the temperature ratcheting. A clutch engagement dissipates the slip energy ∫ T (ωe − ωl) dt in the friction faces, which for constant torques is T·Δω·tslip/2.

    Fade

    Above its fade temperature the resin in an organic lining out-gasses and μ collapses: here μ falls smoothly to about a third of its cold value. Torque capacity falls with it, so the stop takes longer, absorbs the same energy over more time, and the next stop starts hotter. A drum brake suffers more because its leading shoe multiplies μ.

    Drum brakes

    Nleading = F k /(1 − μ q)  ·  Ntrailing = F k /(1 + μ q)  ·  brake factor = T / (F r)

    The friction force on the leading shoe rotates it harder into the drum (self-energising), so the drum gives more torque per newton of actuation than a disc: a high brake factor. The price is sensitivity: torque swings with μ, water and heat, and as μq → 1 the shoe self-locks.

    Clutch engagement

    Je ω̇e = Tengine − Tc  ·  Jl ω̇l = Tc − Tload  ·  slip time ≈ Δω / [(Tc − Te)/Je + (Tc − TL)/Jl]

    While the plates slip the clutch transmits its full capacity: the engine droops and the load spins up until the speeds meet and the plates lock. Then the clutch carries only what the load needs. If the capacity is below the load torque the plates never lock, all the engine power goes into the faces and the clutch burns.

    About the numbers

    [Illustrative data, for training only.] Lining friction, fade temperatures and allowable pressures are typical values; fade is a smooth curve to 35 % of cold; the drum uses a lever ratio 1.6 and self-energising ratio 1.25; rotor specific heat 480 J/kg·K; cooling is a simple speed-dependent coefficient. Real designs use test data for the specific friction pair.

    How to use the demonstrator

    • Choose disc brake, drum brake or plate clutch, then set the actuation (pressure and bore, or spring force), the lining geometry and material, and the inertia or load.
    • Apply brake / Engage clutch runs one event: the transient chart shows speed, torque and lining temperature, the readouts give stop or slip time, energy and temperature rise. Auto-repeat runs events back to back so the lining heats and fades. The clock speeds the thermal history.
    • Bars on the right show lining temperature against the fade point and friction as a share of cold friction.
    • Record readings to compare distributions, pressures, disc against drum and cold against faded in the tasks. Every completed event is also logged.
