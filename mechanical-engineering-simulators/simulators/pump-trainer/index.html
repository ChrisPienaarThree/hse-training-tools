    Head from the two pressure gauges

    H = (pd − ps)/ρg + (vd² − vs²)/2g + Δz

    The rig's gauges sit on equal-bore flanges at the same height, so head is simply the pressure difference in metres of the fluid actually in the loop — density matters when you switch to glycol.

    Power and efficiency

    Phyd = ρgQH  ·  ηpump = Phyd/Pshaft  ·  ηoverall = Phyd/Pelec  ·  Pelec = √3·V·I·cos φ

    Motor current and power are what you can measure on a real board; shaft power needs the motor's efficiency, which falls at part load. The efficiency curve is built from recorded points, not read off a nameplate.

    Affinity laws

    Q₂/Q₁ = n₂/n₁  ·  H₂/H₁ = (n₂/n₁)²  ·  P₂/P₁ = (n₂/n₁)³

    Record the H–Q curve at 100 %, then at 80 % and 60 %. Points recorded at a fixed throttle across speeds fall on the system curve, not on an affinity curve — a common confusion the chart makes visible.

    Series and parallel

    Parallel: Q(H) = Q₁(H) + Q₂(H)  ·  Series: H(Q) = H₁(Q) + H₂(Q)

    Parallel pumps add flow at equal head; series pumps add head at equal flow. Against a steep system curve, a second pump in parallel adds far less than double the flow; against a flat one, series adds little head. Run P2 slower than P1 to see one pump being pushed off its curve.

    Bearings, vibration and temperature

    Bearing temperature follows speed and load with a time constant of minutes; vibration follows speed and rises sharply with cavitation or off-curve operation. In a closed loop nearly all shaft power ends up as heat in the fluid, so a long dead-headed run warms the tank — use the thermal ×20 clock to see it in class time.

    How to use the trainer

    • Curves are built, not shown. The chart starts with only your recorded points. Vary the discharge throttle at fixed speed and record to build the H–Q and efficiency curves; vary speed at fixed throttle to build the system curve; repeat at other speeds for affinity; switch to parallel or series and repeat.
    • Reveal model curves only to check your construction.
    • Sweep discharge throttle records nine points automatically at the current speed and arrangement.
    • Instruments: every reading a real board would give — suction and discharge pressure, flow, rpm, motor current and power, bearing vibration and temperature, fluid temperature. Head and efficiencies are derived and shown in the log so learners can check their own arithmetic.
    • Thermal ×20 speeds up bearing and fluid heating so a dead-head or a long run shows within a lesson.
