    Why a PD pump needs a relief valve

    QPD ≈ Vd · N · (1 − slip)  — nearly independent of pressure

    A centrifugal pump against a closed valve simply climbs to its shut-off head and stays there. A positive-displacement pump keeps delivering the same volume every stroke, so the pressure rises until something gives: the relief valve, the hose, the coupling or the motor. That is why a relief valve on the pump side of any isolation is mandatory, not optional.

    Pulsation and the dampener

    A single-acting diaphragm pump delivers in half-sine pulses and nothing on the suction stroke. A gas-charged dampener stores the peak and releases it between strokes; it works best charged to about 80 % of line pressure. Uncharged or isolated, the pressure ripple reappears, the gauge becomes unreadable and hoses fatigue.

    Back-pressure valve

    Metering accuracy depends on the check valves seating cleanly. A back-pressure valve holds a minimum discharge pressure so the pump always works against a positive head, even into an open tank or a siphoning line.

    Calibration column

    ηvol = Qmeasured / (Vd · N · stroke)

    Timing the level in a graduated column is the standard field check of a metering pump. Volumetric efficiency falls as discharge pressure rises because slip past the check valves increases.

    Torque and current

    T ∝ Vd · Δp  ·  Pshaft ∝ Q · Δp

    PD pump torque is proportional to differential pressure and independent of speed, so an overpressure shows up first as rising motor current and finally as a stall.

    About the skid data

    [Illustrative skid data, for training only.] Diaphragm pump 50 mL/stroke, 20–120 strokes/min, single-acting; gear pump 8 mL/rev, 300–1 500 rpm. Relief valve adjustable 2–12 bar; hose rated 10 bar; motor stalls at about 14 bar; 500 mL calibration column. The comparison centrifugal has 3.4 bar shut-off and about 29 L/min run-out.

    How to use the skid

    • Start the pump and watch the pulses travel down the transparent discharge line. Switch between diaphragm and gear to compare pulsation.
    • Close the discharge valve progressively — by hand or with the demonstration button — and watch the PD pressure climb while the centrifugal trace flattens at its shut-off head. The relief valve lifts at its set pressure and returns flow to the tank.
    • Isolate the relief valve and close the discharge again: the hose ruptures or the motor stalls. Reset to repair.
    • Dampener: isolate it, or set its charge far from line pressure, and read the ripple on the trace and the gauge.
    • Calibration column: draw from the column for a timed period and compare measured flow with the theoretical displacement.
