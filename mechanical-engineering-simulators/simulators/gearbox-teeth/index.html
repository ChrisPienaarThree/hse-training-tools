    Tooth forces

    Wt = 2T / d  ·  Wr = Wt tan αt  ·  Wa = Wt tan β  ·  d = m z / cos β

    Torque over the pitch radius gives the tangential force that transmits the power. The 20° pressure angle adds a radial component that pushes the gears apart, and a helix adds an axial thrust. All three land on the bearings; the axial one needs a bearing that can take it.

    Bending and contact stress

    σb = Wt Ko Kv Km / (b m Y)  ·  σH = Cp √[Wt Ko Kv Km / (b d₁ I)]

    The Lewis equation treats the tooth as a cantilever; the form factor Y grows with tooth number. The Hertz equation gives the surface pressure between the two curved flanks; it rises only with the square root of load but is compared with a much higher allowable. Steel gears are usually limited by contact (pitting), not by bending. Kv accounts for tooth impacts at speed and gets worse with poorer quality.

    Efficiency

    Pmesh = P μ Hv  ·  Hv = π (u + 1)/(z₁ u cos β) (1 − εα + εα²/2)

    Tooth sliding costs a fraction of the transmitted power that depends on friction and on the tooth numbers (finer pitch, more teeth, less sliding). Churning, bearings and seals are largely fixed losses, so they dominate at part load and at high speed: a gearbox at 20 % load is much less efficient than at full load, and a fast gearbox in thick oil churns.

    Lubrication failures

    The oil film between the flanks must be thicker than the surface roughness (λ above about 1). Thin, hot oil, high load and high sliding raise the flash temperature and can weld and tear the surfaces: scuffing, in seconds. Contact stress above the allowable does not break anything at once; it fatigues the surface and pits it over hours, in proportion to a steep power of the overload.

    About the numbers

    [Illustrative data, for training only.] Lewis form factors and an AGMA-style dynamic factor; allowable stresses typical of the material classes; friction, churning and film-thickness relations are simplified; the oil is a single thermal mass. Real gear rating uses the full ISO 6336 or AGMA 2001 factors.

    How to use the bench

    • Geometry: module, teeth, face width, helix and quality. The drawing, forces and stresses update at once; the stress chart shows both stresses against power with their allowables.
    • Drive: speed, power and service factor. The efficiency chart shows where you sit on the load curve and how the losses split.
    • Run the clock to heat the oil: viscosity falls, friction and film ratio change, and overloads use up pitting life or scuff the teeth. New gears and oil resets damage and temperature.
    • Record readings to compare module, face width, helix, load and speed in the tasks.
