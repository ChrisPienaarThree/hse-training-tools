    Friction drives

    T₁ / T₂ = e^{μθ}  (flat)  ·  T₁ / T₂ = e^{μθ / sin(β/2)}  (V, β = 38°)  ·  Te = T₁ − T₂  ·  P = Te · v

    A belt transmits the difference between its tight and slack side tensions. The Euler–Eytelwein ratio caps how unequal they can be for a given friction and wrap; the V groove multiplies the effective friction about three times, which is why a V-belt carries several times the power of a flat belt at the same tension.

    Installation tension

    T₁ + T₂ = 2T₀  ·  capacity Te,max = 2(T₀ − Tc) (e^{μθ} − 1)/(e^{μθ} + 1)

    Tension sets the capacity. Too loose and the drive slips under load, squeals and burns the belt; too tight and the shaft and bearings carry T₁ + T₂ for nothing while the belt fatigues. Centrifugal tension Tc = m v² uses up tension at high belt speed and carries no load.

    Creep and slip

    Even below the limit a belt creeps 0.5–2 % because it stretches on the tight side and relaxes on the slack side, so the ratio is never exactly the diameter ratio. Past the limit it slips grossly: the driven speed falls, the belt heats, and life collapses. Synchronous belts and chains do not creep; they jump teeth instead when tension or wear lets the belt ride up.

    Wrap angle and ratio

    θ₁ ≈ 180° − 2 sin⁻¹((d₂ − d₁)/2C)

    A big ratio on a short centre distance leaves the small pulley with little wrap, and capacity falls with it. The cure is a longer centre distance, an idler, or a two-stage drive.

    Chains

    speed variation = 1 − cos(180°/z)  ·  chain pull = 2T/d

    A chain wraps a polygon, not a circle, so the chain speed pulses every tooth: 4 % on an 11-tooth sprocket, under 1 % on 19 teeth. That pulse is vibration and wear. Chains want at least 17 teeth on the small sprocket, lubrication chosen by speed, and replacement at about 3 % elongation before the chain rides up the teeth.

    About the numbers

    [Illustrative data, for training only.] Belt sections carry a rated power per belt that rises with belt speed and falls for small pulleys; service factor 1.3. Belt temperature comes from slip power on a thermal clock; life factor from temperature and misalignment. Timing belt and chain jump limits are simplified.

    How to use the bench

    • Choose the drive: V-belt, flat belt, synchronous belt or roller chain, then pulley or sprocket sizes, centre distance, section and number of belts.
    • Run it: set the driver speed and the load torque. Read the tensions on the drawing, the slip, the driven speed and the power. Raise the load until the belt slips, or lower the installation tension.
    • Chart: transmitted tension and slip against load, with the capacity line for the present tension and wrap. For a chain the chart shows the chordal speed pulse over one tooth.
    • Record readings to compare tension, wrap, V against flat and belt count in the tasks.
