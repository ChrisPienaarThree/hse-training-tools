    Force is pressure times area

    F = p · A  ·  Acap = πD²/4  ·  Arod side = π(D² − d²)/4

    The pump does not make force; it makes flow. The relief valve sets the maximum pressure, and the piston area turns that pressure into force. Double the bore and the force goes up four times at the same pressure. The load cell on the rod reads what the load actually feels: the pressure force less seal friction, or, when stalled, whatever the relief setting allows.

    Speed is flow divided by area

    v = Q / A  ·  Phydraulic = p · Q  (bar · L/min / 600 = kW)

    The same flow that moves a 25 mm piston at a third of a metre per second crawls an 80 mm piston at a thirtieth. Force and speed trade against each other at constant power: the trade-off chart shows both for every bore in the rack.

    The rod side

    Retracting, the pressure acts on the annulus, so the force is lower and the speed higher by the ratio Acap/Arod side. Block the rod-side return while extending and the cap-side pressure is multiplied by that same ratio on the rod side: a 50/28 cylinder at 150 bar can see 220 bar on its rod seal. That is pressure intensification, and it is why a meter-out valve must never be fully closed on a pressurised cylinder.

    Where the power goes

    Motor power is pump pressure times pump flow. Useful power is load force times rod speed. Everything in between is heat: flow over the relief valve at full pressure, the drop across a meter-in valve, the drop across a meter-out valve. Throttling a fixed-displacement pump to half speed wastes more than half the motor power in the oil, and the tank shows it (thermal clock ×10 here so the rise is visible in minutes).

    Overrunning loads

    Lowering a weight, the load drives the cylinder faster than the pump can fill the cap side unless the meter-out valve holds it back. When it runs away the cap side voids and the pressure gauge drops to zero: cavitation, followed by a bang when the void collapses.

    About the rig data

    [Illustrative data, for training only.] 4 kW power pack, 8 cm³/rev gear pump on a variable-speed motor (10.7 L/min at 1 450 rpm), relief 20–250 bar, tandem-centre or closed-centre 4/3 valve, needle valves meter-in and meter-out, 30 L tank. Cylinder rack: bores 25–80 mm with standard rods, 300 mm stroke, rated 250 bar, rod-seal limit 320 bar. Seal friction 3 % plus 150 N. Return-line back-pressure 1.5 bar.

    How to use the demonstrator

    • Pick a cylinder from the rack (bore and rod). The drawing scales with it and the piston-face inset shows the two areas.
    • Start the pump, set the relief valve, and hold Extend or Retract. Read P1 on the pack, P2 and P3 at the ports, and the force at the rod on the load cell. Compare it with p·A.
    • Change the load: a dead weight, a spring that stiffens with stroke, or a brake that never yields. Stall the cylinder and watch the pressure sit at the relief setting.
    • Valves: meter-in wastes power at the relief valve; meter-out controls overrunning loads but intensifies rod-side pressure when closed.
    • Record readings at several bores and let the tasks check them.
