      Reading the map

      Each speed line is the pressure ratio the compressor can produce against inlet flow at that speed. The line ends on the left at the surge line (the peak of the curve) and on the right at the choke line, where the gas reaches sonic velocity in the narrowest passage and no more flow will pass. The compressor can only run on the falling part between them. Where the operating point sits on that line is decided by the process: the intersection with the system line.

      Why surge happens

      Left of the peak the curve slopes the wrong way: less flow gives less pressure. The discharge volume is still at the higher pressure, so it blows back through the machine, flow reverses, the discharge pressure collapses, forward flow re-establishes near choke, the volume recharges and the cycle repeats every few seconds. Every reversal loads the thrust bearing backwards and heats the gas. A few cycles are survivable; sustained surge is not.

      dm/dt = (A/L)·(Pc(m) − Pplenum)  ·  dPplenum/dt = (a²/V)·(m − mprocess − mrecycle)

      Four ways to walk into surge

      Discharge pressure up: the header rises, the point slides up and left along the speed line. Process demand down: less flow taken, same effect. Suction pressure down: the required pressure ratio Pd/Ps rises even though nothing downstream changed. Speed down: the whole curve shrinks (flow ∝ N, head ∝ N²) and can no longer meet the header. Watch the operating point: in every case it moves toward the surge line.

      Surge control

      The anti-surge controller measures flow (orifice ΔP) and pressures, not speed, and computes the distance to the surge line. It holds the point on a surge control line a margin to the right, typically about 10 % in flow, by opening the recycle valve so the compressor moves more gas than the process takes. Two protections sit behind the PI action: a quick-open step when flow falls faster than a process can explain, and a surge counter that trips the machine if the valve is losing.

      SM = (Q − Qsurge) / Qsurge  ·  Qsurge ∝ N  ·  headsurge ∝ N²

      Valve response time is the whole game

      A disturbance moves the point toward surge in seconds; a surge cycle lasts two to five seconds. An anti-surge valve is specified to stroke fully open in one to two seconds, with a slower close so the controller does not chase it. Set the stroke to 15 s in this simulator and the identical disturbance surges the machine several times before the recycle catches up; at 2 s it never crosses the line.

      What the vibration probe sees

      Approaching surge, rotating stall cells make the shaft vibration rise before anything else changes. Each surge cycle gives a large, broadband, low-frequency burst as the flow reverses and the thrust load flips. Near choke the flow separates and vibration rises again, more gently.       About the machine data

      [Illustrative data, for training only.] Single-stage centrifugal process compressor, nitrogen-like gas (M = 28, k = 1.4), design 8 000 m³/h actual inlet flow, pressure ratio 2.4 at 10 000 rpm and 5 bar a suction, about 1.4 MW; polytropic efficiency 0.80 at best point. Speed lines follow the fan laws; the curve shape is a Moore–Greitzer cubic with a stonewall. Discharge volume 8 m³. All numbers are chosen to show the mechanisms clearly, not measured on any real machine.

      How to use the simulator

      • Start the compressor. It runs up with the recycle valve open and the anti-surge controller (ASC) closes the valve as the process takes the flow. Watch the operating point on the map settle right of the surge control line.
      • Push it toward surge with any of the four sliders: suction pressure down, header pressure up, speed down, process demand down. The status pill changes from normal to approaching surge as the point enters the control band, and to surge when the flow reverses.
      • Recycle valve response sets how fast the anti-surge valve can move. Try the same scenario at 2 s and at 15 s. Put the ASC in Manual to protect the machine yourself, or to let it surge.
      • Traces show discharge and suction pressure, inlet flow with the surge control flow, recycle valve position and shaft vibration, with a phase band along the top. A vibration trip or the surge counter stops the machine.
      • Scenarios under the controls ramp the process for you; "Surge sequence" plays normal → approaching → surge → recovery in about a minute.
