    The equation

    m ẍ + c ẋ + k x = F(t)  ·  ωn = √(k/m)  ·  ζ = c / 2√(km)  ·  ωd = ωn√(1 − ζ²)

    Everything on this bench comes from one second-order equation, integrated numerically in real time. Mass sets the inertia, the spring stores energy, the damper drains it. Stiffer or lighter means faster; more damping means the free motion dies sooner and the resonance peak is lower.

    Free vibration

    x = X e−ζωnt cos(ωdt − φ)  ·  δ = ln(xn/xn+1) = 2πζ/√(1 − ζ²)

    Release the mass and count peaks: the period gives fd, the ratio of successive peaks gives the logarithmic decrement and hence ζ. Critical damping (ζ = 1) returns to rest fastest without overshoot; overdamped creeps back.

    Harmonic force

    X k / F₀ = 1 / √((1 − r²)² + (2ζr)²)  ·  tan φ = 2ζr / (1 − r²)

    Below resonance the spring carries the force and the mass follows in phase. At r = 1 the peak is 1/2ζ and the response lags by 90°. Above resonance inertia dominates, the amplitude falls toward zero and the phase goes to 180°.

    Base excitation and isolation

    X / Y = √(1 + (2ζr)²) / √((1 − r²)² + (2ζr)²)

    The same curve gives the force transmitted to a foundation by a machine on springs. Transmissibility is below 1 only for r > √2, so an isolator must be soft enough that its natural frequency sits well below the excitation. Above √2 extra damping makes isolation worse, not better.

    Sweeping

    The stepped sine holds each frequency until the transient has died (about four time constants) and records the settled amplitude ratio, so the measured points can be compared with the theoretical curve. The half-power bandwidth around the peak gives ζ ≈ Δf / 2fn.

    About the rig

    [Illustrative data, for training only.] Mass plates 0.5–20 kg, spring 50–5 000 N/m, damper 0–400 N·s/m, travel ±120 mm to the stops (restitution 0.5 on impact), shaker base ±40 mm, force actuator ±100 N. Integration: fourth-order Runge–Kutta at 2 400 steps per second.

    How to use the demonstrator

    • Set the system: mass, spring, damper (or pick ζ directly). The readouts give fn, ζ, fd, time constant and log decrement.
    • Choose the excitation: release from a displacement, drive with a harmonic force, shake the base, apply a step, or tap it with an impulse. Press Start.
    • Read the traces: the envelope and zero crossings give the measured frequency and decrement; the frequency-response chart shows where you are on the curve and the sweep plots measured points over it.
    • Slow the clock to watch fast systems; Record readings to compare cases in the tasks.

    Keys: Space start / stop · R record · 1–5 excitation mode · Esc close
