    Rankine cycle

    wt = h₁ − h₂  ·  wp = v Δp / ηp  ·  qin = h₁ − h₄  ·  η = (wt − wp) / qin

    Water is pumped to boiler pressure (almost no work, it is a liquid), boiled and superheated, expanded through the turbine, and condensed. Efficiency rises with boiler pressure and superheat because heat is added at a higher average temperature, and with condenser vacuum because heat is rejected at a lower one. The limit is Carnot between the same extremes; the gap is the heat added while the water warms up and boils well below the peak temperature.

    Wet exhaust and reheat

    Expanding from high pressure without enough superheat lands the exhaust inside the dome. Below about 88 % quality the water droplets erode the last blade rows. Reheat takes the partly expanded steam back to the boiler and dries the exhaust while adding heat at high temperature, so it helps both problems. The isentropic efficiency of the turbine also dries the exhaust, but by wasting work as heat.

    Brayton cycle

    T₂ = T₁ [1 + (rp^{(k−1)/k} − 1)/ηc]  ·  T₄ = T₃ [1 − ηt(1 − rp^{−(k−1)/k})]  ·  η = (wt − wc) / cp(T₃ − T₂)

    Air is compressed, heated by burning fuel, and expanded. The compressor eats half or more of the turbine work (the back-work ratio), so component efficiencies matter far more than in a steam plant. Efficiency keeps rising with pressure ratio, but net work per kg peaks at rp = (T₃/T₁ · ηcηt)^{k/2(k−1)}; turbine inlet temperature is the lever, limited by blade materials and cooling.

    Regenerator and combined cycle

    At low pressure ratio the exhaust is hotter than the compressor delivery, so a heat exchanger can preheat the air with exhaust heat and cut fuel. At high pressure ratio it is useless. The combined cycle instead uses the hot exhaust to raise steam in a heat recovery steam generator and runs a Rankine cycle on it: the gas turbine takes the high temperatures, the steam cycle the low ones, and together they pass 55–60 %.

    About the numbers

    [Illustrative data, for training only.] Steam properties come from a saturation table with an effective superheat heat capacity per pressure, within a few percent of full steam tables; the gas cycle uses the cold air standard (k 1.4, cp 1.005) with no pressure loss; the HRSG recovers exhaust heat down to a 110 °C stack at 92 % with a 25 K approach. Real plant uses full property routines, pressure losses, bleed steam and cooling air.

    How to use the board

    • Choose the Rankine steam cycle, the Brayton gas turbine or the combined cycle. The flow board shows the state points with temperature, pressure, enthalpy and quality.
    • Move the sliders: boiler pressure, superheat, condenser pressure, reheat, component efficiencies, pressure ratio, turbine inlet temperature, regenerator. Everything updates at once, including the T-s diagram and the sweep chart.
    • Sweep chart: efficiency against the main design variable with the present setting marked, so you can see where you sit on the curve.
    • Record readings to compare cycles in the tasks.

    Keys: Space flow / pause · R record · 1–3 cycle · Esc close
