    Reactions first

    A simply supported beam is solved by moments about one support. Fixed ends and propped cantilevers are statically indeterminate: the extra reactions come from compatibility (the fixed end cannot rotate, the prop cannot move), which is what the solver here does with beam finite elements. The diagrams are then built by statics from the reactions, so they are exact whichever support arrangement you choose.

    The standard results

    Point load mid-span: M = PL/4, δ = PL³/48EI  ·  UDL: M = wL²/8, δ = 5wL⁴/384EI

    Cantilever tip load: M = PL, δ = PL³/3EI  ·  Fixed ends, UDL: Msupport = wL²/12, Mmid = wL²/24, δ = wL⁴/384EI

    Use them to check the bench, then break them: move a support, add an overhang, change the section.

    Shear and moment

    Shear is the running sum of the vertical forces from the left; the moment is the running integral of the shear. A point load steps the shear; a couple steps the moment; a distributed load makes the shear slope and the moment curve. The moment is greatest where the shear crosses zero, and a point of contraflexure is where the moment itself changes sign.

    Stress and section

    σ = M / Z  ·  Z = I / ymax  ·  Irect = bh³/12

    Doubling the depth of a rectangle multiplies I by eight and Z by four. An I-section puts the material where the stress is, far from the neutral axis, which is why it beats a solid rectangle of the same mass many times over.

    Deflection and stiffness

    EI v″ = M(x)

    Deflection depends on E and I, not on strength. Aluminium at the same stress deflects three times as much as steel; timber twelve times. Serviceability limits (span/250 here, adjustable) often govern before the material yields.

    Overhangs and uplift

    Moving the supports in from the ends of a uniformly loaded beam reduces the mid-span moment while building a hogging moment over the supports; at about 0.207 L overhang the two are equal and the peak moment is at its lowest. Load an overhang heavily and the far support goes into uplift: a roller cannot hold the beam down.

    About the numbers

    [Illustrative data, for training only.] Sections are parametric; allowable stress is fy/1.5; no lateral-torsional buckling, shear deflection, or dynamic effects. 200 beam elements; loads are distributed consistently to the nodes.

    How to use the beam bench

    • Set the beam: span, support arrangement and support positions. Supports can be dragged along the beam for overhangs.
    • Add loads: point loads, distributed loads and couples from the load list. Drag the red point-load arrows and the support triangles on the drawing.
    • Read reactions on the drawing, the extremes in the readouts, and the shear, moment and deflection diagrams on the right. The deflected shape is drawn exaggerated on the beam.
    • Section and material change stress and deflection but not the moment: compare a rectangle with an I-section, steel with aluminium.
    • Record readings and let the tasks check them against the standard results.
