    What a derrick is

    A mast held upright by two stiff legs (struts to anchored sills) or by guys, and a boom pinned at the mast foot and luffed by a topping lift from the mast head. The load hangs from the boom tip; the hoist rope runs from the tip down the boom to a winch at the foot. A stiff-leg derrick can slew through roughly 270°: the boom cannot pass the legs. Everything here is static equilibrium in the plane of the boom, plus the two legs in plan.

    Topping lift

    Ttopping = (W + Wboom/2) · dtopping / (Hmast − hfoot)

    Moments about the boom foot. The topping-rope length d grows and the mast height sits in the denominator, so a taller mast or a shorter, steeper boom eases the topping lift. Divide by the parts of line for the rope tension and the winch pull.

    Boom compression

    Cboom = Ttopping·(component along the boom) + W·sin θ + W/nhoist + Wboom·sin θ

    The topping lift squeezes the boom, the load's weight has a component along it, and the hoist lead rope pulls the tip toward the foot with the rope tension. The strut capacity falls with the square of the length (Euler), so a longer boom buys radius with strength.

    Mast and legs

    The topping lift pulls the mast head toward the boom tip. The horizontal part must be held by the legs; in plan the two leg forces are solved from that horizontal pull at the current slew. Slew toward one leg and it takes nearly all of it; slew past the rear quarter and the other leg goes into compression. The vertical parts of the leg forces, the topping-lift rope leading down the mast, and the downward pull of the topping lift add up to the mast compression.

    Anchors and overturning

    A leg in tension lifts its anchor with T·Hmast/Lleg. When that exceeds the hold-down (dead weight, ballast or bolts) the sill lifts and the derrick overturns about the mast foot. The base bearing carries the load, the boom and the anchors' uplift.

    Load chart

    For every radius the rated load is the smallest load at which any limit is reached: boom strut, topping rope at safety factor 5, hoist rope or winch pull, anchor hold-down, leg strut, mast. The chart is recomputed whenever a dimension changes; the colour shows what governs.

    About the rig data

    [Illustrative data, for training only.] Member capacities scale with (20 m / length)² from the values you set. Ropes use the marked breaking load divided by 5. Winch pull and anchor hold-down are single numbers. No dynamic factors, wind, side load or sling geometry: real lifts add all of them.

    How to use the demonstrator

    • Set the rig with the dimension sliders: mast height, boom length and foot height, sill reach, leg spread. Change rigging: parts of line, rope sizes, block mass, anchor hold-down, strut capacities, winch pull.
    • Operate it: hoist to lift the load off the ground, luff the boom out and in, slew left and right. Members recolour with utilisation; the readouts give every force.
    • Load chart shows the rated load at every radius for the rig as it is now, coloured by what governs, with the current lift as a red dot. Utilisation bars show how close each part is to its limit.
    • Record readings to compare reeving, mast height, boom angle, boom length and slew. The tasks check the comparisons.
