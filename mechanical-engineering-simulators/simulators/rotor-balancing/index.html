    Critical speed

    kshaft = 3 E I L / (a² b²)  ·  1/k = 1/kshaft + 1/(2 kbearing)  ·  ωn = √(k/m)

    A disc on a flexible shaft is a mass on a spring. The shaft stiffness depends on its diameter to the fourth power and the span to the third; flexible bearings or pedestals add in series and lower the critical speed. The first critical is where the running speed meets that natural frequency.

    Response to unbalance

    r = e (ω/ωn)² / √[(1 − (ω/ωn)²)² + (2ζ ω/ωn)²]  ·  tan φ = 2ζ(ω/ωn) / (1 − (ω/ωn)²)

    Below the critical the disc whirls with the heavy spot outside (phase near 0°); at the critical the amplitude is limited only by damping (1/2ζ times the eccentricity) and the high spot lags the heavy spot by 90°; well above it the disc turns about its mass centre and the shaft whirls with amplitude equal to the eccentricity, heavy spot inside (180°). Getting through fast, with enough damping, is how supercritical machines start.

    Single-plane balancing

    T = (O + T) − O  ·  Ucorr = −O · Utrial / T

    The original vibration vector O is caused by the unknown unbalance. A known trial mass changes it to O + T; the difference T is the response to the trial mass alone and gives the influence coefficient (vibration per unit unbalance, with its phase). The correction is the unbalance that would produce −O. It only works if the trial mass changes the reading enough (30 % in amplitude or 30° in phase) and both readings are at the same speed.

    Balance grade

    G = eper · ω  (mm/s)  ·  e = U / m

    ISO 21940 grades express permissible residual unbalance as the velocity of the mass centre: G6.3 for general machinery, G2.5 for turbines and pumps, G1 for precision spindles. At higher speed the same eccentricity gives a worse grade, so fast rotors must be balanced finer.

    About the numbers

    [Illustrative data, for training only.] A single-mass Jeffcott rotor with viscous damping and identical isotropic bearings; the probe reads the disc displacement; no gyroscopic effects, no second mode, no bearing anisotropy. The unknown unbalance is generated at random for each balancing job.

    How to use the rig

    • Rotor: set disc mass, shaft diameter, span, disc position, bearing stiffness and damping. The critical speed updates at once.
    • Run-up sweep drives the speed up through the critical and back; watch the Bode plot fill, the orbit grow and the phase swing. The trip limit stops the drive if the vibration is too high.
    • Balancing job: at a speed well below the critical take the original reading, fit the trial mass, take the trial reading, use the computed correction (or compute your own from the polar plot), fit it and check the residual.
    • Record readings to compare damping, supports and speeds in the tasks.
