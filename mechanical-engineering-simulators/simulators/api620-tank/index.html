    What API 620 covers

    Large welded tanks that store liquids with a gas or vapour space at up to 15 psig (103.4 kPa gauge) and metal temperatures up to 121 °C: refrigerated ammonia, LPG, ethylene and LNG, blanketed process liquids, volatile products. Below 2.5 psig (18 kPa) API 650 Annex F can also be used; above 15 psig the tank is a pressure vessel under a vessel code. Annex R covers refrigerated products from −51 °C to +4 °C in impact-tested carbon steels; Annex Q covers −51 °C down to −168 °C in 9 % nickel steel, stainless steel and aluminium.

    Membrane unit forces (5.10.2)

    T1 = (R2/2) (P + W/At)  ·  T2 = R2 (P − T1/R1)

    At any level of a surface of revolution the meridional force per unit length T1 comes from vertical equilibrium of everything above the level: pressure on the horizontal area At plus or minus the weight W of metal and liquid. The latitudinal (hoop) force T2 then follows from the two radii of curvature: R1 along the meridian and R2 normal to the surface out to the axis. For the cylindrical sidewall R1 is infinite so T2 = P R, with P the gas pressure plus the liquid head at that level; T1 = (R/2)(Pg − W/At) carries the gas pressure on the roof less the metal above. For a spherical dome R1 = R2 = Rc and T1 = T2 = P Rc/2. For a cone R2 = R/sin θ, so a shallow cone has a large hoop force.

    Thickness for tension and compression (5.10.3)

    t = T / (Sts E) + c  ·  compression: T/(t − c) ≤ Sca  ·  Sca = 12 400 (t − c)/R up to 82.7 MPa, then rising to 103.4 MPa at (t − c)/R = 0.0175

    A tensile unit force needs plate area T/(S E) where Sts is the lesser of 30 % of tensile strength and 60 % of yield and E is the joint efficiency from the radiography. A compressive unit force is limited by buckling, so the allowable compressive stress depends on the thickness-to-radius ratio, using the radius in the direction of the compression. Each element is designed for both load cases here: design pressure with the tank full, and the vacuum case with live or snow load and the weight of the metal on an empty tank.

    The knuckle and the compression ring (5.12)

    knuckle: T2 = R (P − T1/rk) < 0 when rk < R/2  ·  ring: Q = R T1r cos φ  ·  Ac = Q / 103.4 MPa  ·  wc = 0.6 √(Rc tc), wh = 0.6 √(R ts)

    Where the roof meets the sidewall the roof pulls inward. In a torispherical roof the small knuckle radius rk makes the hoop force there negative: the knuckle is in compression under internal pressure and its plate is sized by buckling — the signature API 620 result. With a dome or cone that meets the sidewall at an angle, the inward pull becomes a hoop compression Q in a ring at the junction. The ring region may count a width of roof and sidewall plate near the joint, and the rest must be added as an angle or bar until the area reaches Q divided by the 103.4 MPa (15 000 psi) compression allowable.

    Uplift and anchorage (5.11)

    U = Pg π R² − Wmetal  ·  bolts N ≥ U / (Sb As), spacing ≤ 3 m  ·  test at 1.25 Pg

    Gas pressure pushes up on the whole roof area. With the tank empty only the metal holds the shell down, so any pressure beyond a few kilopascals needs anchor bolts around the base, checked at design pressure and at the 1.25 × test pressure with the higher test allowable. The bolt count also follows from the maximum spacing. A blanketed tank at 35 kPa on a 30 m diameter carries 25 MN of uplift.

    The vapour space and the vent

    Pabs Vgas = constant  ·  PV valve at +Pg / −Pe

    Filling compresses the vapour space and pumping out expands it; the pressure-vacuum valve relieves at the set points so the tank stays inside its design envelope. A blocked or frozen vent turns a pump-out into a vacuum in seconds and a fill into overpressure — the two classic ways a low-pressure tank is destroyed. The demonstrator models the vapour space isothermally, lets you block the vent, and fails the element that first passes 1.5 × its allowable.

    About the numbers

    [Illustrative data, for training only.] Allowable stresses are derived from the 30 % Fu / 60 % Fy rule rather than read from the tables; the Annex Q allowables, minimum design metal temperatures, joint efficiencies, the compressive-stress curve, the compression-ring participating widths, the anchor bolt allowables and the test factor follow the SI forms as commonly taught and must be checked against the edition of API 620 in force. Biaxial stress interaction (Figure 5-1), roof framing, insulation and the outer tank of a double-wall refrigerated tank, nozzles, seismic and wind are outside this demonstrator. Nothing here replaces a design by a competent engineer.

    How to use the demonstrator

    • Define the tank: diameter, courses, liquid level, product (which sets gravity, temperature and a typical gas pressure), then pressure, vacuum, material, radiography and corrosion allowance. Every plate, the ring, uplift and the checks update at once.
    • Read the forces: the first chart follows T1 and T2 from the apex of the roof down to the bottom for the pressure case (solid) and the vacuum case (dashed). Shaded values are compression.
    • Roof choice: dome, torispherical or cone. The knuckle or the compression ring appears on the drawing; set the ring section with its slider until the region passes.
    • Override: click a course, the roof or the knuckle, then Thicker / Thinner or the arrow keys. Auto returns to the calculated plate.
    • Operate: Fill, Pump out, Pressurise, the hydro-pneumatic test (water to 1.25 × G × H, then air to 1.25 × Pg, utilisation shown against 1.25 × the allowable), or block the vent and watch the vapour-space pressure on the gauge. Elements turn hot as they pass their allowable and the tank fails at 1.5 ×.
