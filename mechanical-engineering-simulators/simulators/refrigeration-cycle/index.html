    The cycle

    qe = h₁ − h₄  ·  w = h₂ − h₁  ·  COP = qe / w  ·  h₄ = h₃ (throttling)

    Liquid refrigerant flashes through the expansion valve to the evaporating pressure, boils in the evaporator taking heat from the cold space, is compressed to the condensing pressure and gives that heat plus the compressor work to the ambient in the condenser. On the p-h diagram the cycle is a quadrilateral whose width at the bottom is the cooling effect and whose slanted side is the compressor work.

    Temperatures set everything

    COPCarnot = Te / (Tc − Te)

    A higher condensing temperature (hot day, fouled condenser, undersized condenser) raises the discharge pressure: more work per kg, less cooling per kg, hotter discharge. A lower evaporating temperature thins the suction gas, so the same swept volume moves far less mass: capacity collapses at deep-freeze temperatures. Every kelvin of lift costs 2–3 % of COP.

    Superheat and subcooling

    Superheat at the suction guarantees dry gas at the compressor; without it liquid enters and hammers the valves. Subcooling of the liquid before the valve reduces the flash gas and adds cooling capacity for free. A thermostatic expansion valve holds the superheat; low charge shows as high superheat and low capacity.

    Compressor

    ηv = 1 − c (rp^{1/n} − 1)  ·  ṁ = ηv Vd / v₁

    Gas trapped in the clearance volume re-expands before suction can begin, so the volumetric efficiency falls as the pressure ratio rises. Discharge temperature climbs with pressure ratio and poor isentropic efficiency; above about 120 °C the oil breaks down.

    About the numbers

    [Illustrative data, for training only.] R134a saturation properties from a table with an effective vapour heat capacity per pressure and a simple gas law for the suction volume; the cold room is a lumped thermal mass with a fixed evaporator and condenser approach and a 2 K thermostat differential. Real plant uses full property data, evaporator and condenser heat-transfer models and part-load compressor maps.

    How to use the trainer

    • Cycle explorer: set evaporating and condensing temperatures, superheat, subcooling and compressor data. The circuit, the p-h diagram and the readouts update at once.
    • Cold room: the room starts at ambient; the compressor pulls it down while the evaporating temperature follows the room and the condensing temperature follows the ambient. The thermostat cycles the compressor. The clock compresses hours into minutes.
    • Sweep chart: capacity and COP of this compressor against evaporating temperature at two condensing temperatures, with the present cycle marked.
    • Record readings to compare cycles in the tasks.
