    Where the operating point comes from

    The pump can only run where its own curve meets the system curve. Change the speed and the pump curve moves; change the valve, the pipe or the length and the system curve moves. The intersection is the only steady state.

    Hpump(Q) = Hstatic + (f·L/D + ΣK)·v²/2g + (Q/Kv)²·10⁵/ρg

    Affinity laws (speed change)

    Q ∝ N  ·  H ∝ N²  ·  P ∝ N³

    Halving the speed gives half the flow, a quarter of the head and an eighth of the power. This is why speed control usually beats throttling for part-load duty.

    Pipe friction (Darcy–Weisbach)

    hf = f · (L/D) · v²/2g    v = Q/A    Re = ρvD/μ

    For a fixed flow, the velocity rises with 1/D², so friction loss rises roughly with 1/D⁵. Swapping DN50 for DN25 is not twice as hard to push through: it is about thirty times harder.

    Control valve

    ΔPbar = (Qm³/h / Kv)²    Kv(x) = Kv100 · R(x−1)

    An equal-percentage globe valve: each equal step of stem travel multiplies the flow coefficient by the same ratio. Most of the authority sits in the first third of travel.

    NPSH and cavitation

    NPSHa = patm/ρg + zs − hsuction losses − pvapour/ρg

    Warm fluid raises the vapour pressure; a fouled strainer eats the suction head. When the available margin drops below what the pump requires, vapour bubbles form at the impeller eye and the head collapses.

    About the pump data

    [Illustrative pump data, for training only.] Shut-off head 32 m at 2 900 rpm, best-efficiency point 24 m³/h at 72 %, run-out about 47 m³/h, 4 kW motor. The reservoir is open to atmosphere, so the loop's static head is only the return-pipe outlet height above the free surface.

    How to use the bench

    • Start the pump with the speed slider. Watch the impeller, the flow in the clear pipe and the gauges respond. The motor ramps rather than jumping.
    • Throttle the valve and watch the system curve steepen and the operating point slide up the pump curve to lower flow and higher head.
    • Swap the pipe to a smaller bore: the same effect as throttling, but you cannot undo it from the control room.
    • Reduce speed instead of throttling to reach the same flow and compare the electrical power. That difference is the whole argument for variable-speed drives.
    • Break it on purpose. Shut the valve with the pump running (dead-head), heat the fluid and foul the strainer (cavitation), or open everything with DN50 fitted (run-out and overload). Every failure is shown, not just the success.
    • Record points or auto-sweep to build a table you can copy into a spreadsheet.
