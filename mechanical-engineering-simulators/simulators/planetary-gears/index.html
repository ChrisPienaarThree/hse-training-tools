    Three members, one equation

    ωs Ns + ωr Nr = (Ns + Nr) ωc

    Sun, carrier and ring share one relation, so the train has two degrees of freedom. Hold one member and the other two are geared together at a fixed ratio; drive two and the third takes the weighted average: that is a differential. The planets are the go-between: they never appear in the equation because their tooth count is fixed by geometry, Np = (Nr − Ns)/2.

    The planet as a planet

    Each planet does two things at once: it orbits with the carrier and it spins about its own pin. Its spin relative to the carrier comes from the sun mesh: (ωp − ωc) = −(ωs − ωc) Ns/Np. The marker on one planet traces the sum of the two motions, a looping curve like a moon's path around the sun. The "days per year" readout is the number of spins per orbit.

    Three ways to use it

    Ring held: ωs/ωc = 1 + Nr/Ns  ·  Carrier held: ωs/ωr = −Nr/Ns  ·  Sun held: ωr/ωc = 1 + Ns/Nr

    Same gears, three ratios, one of them reversing. A gearbox selects them with brakes and clutches; lock any two members together and everything turns as one at 1 : 1.

    Torque

    Ts : Tr : Tc = Ns : Nr : −(Ns + Nr)

    Torques on the three members always sum to zero, and the held member takes a reaction: with the ring held and the sun driving, the carrier delivers (1 + Nr/Ns) times the sun torque and the ring bolts see Nr/Ns times it. Power in equals power out less the mesh losses.

    Assembly

    Planets can only be spaced equally if (Ns + Nr) divides by the number of planets; otherwise the teeth do not line up in the gaps. Adjacent planets must also clear each other: the tip circles must not touch. Both checks are shown live.

    The lever analogy

    Plot the three speeds on vertical axes and they lie on a straight line, with the carrier's axis placed Ns/(Ns + Nr) of the way from sun to ring. Pin one point to zero, push another up, and read the third off the line. The tabular method does the same arithmetic: first turn everything together, then hold the carrier and let the simple train do its ratio, then add.

    About the numbers

    [Illustrative data, for training only.] Teeth are drawn as simple trapezoids at the correct pitch and phase so the meshes stay in step; efficiency is applied per mesh; inertia, backlash and load sharing are not modelled.

    How to use the demonstrator

    • Choose the gears: sun and ring tooth counts (the planet count of teeth follows), and how many planets. The assembly check tells you whether they fit.
    • Hold one member and drive another, set the speed and torque. Watch the marker on one planet trace its path while the planet spins and orbits. Slow the animation clock to follow the teeth.
    • Read the numbers: every member's speed and torque, the ratio and direction, planet spins per orbit, power and mesh frequency. The nomograph and the tabular method show where they come from.
    • Differential: hold nothing and drive two members; find the pair of speeds that stops the carrier.
