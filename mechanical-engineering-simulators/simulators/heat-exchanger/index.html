    Two ways to the same answer

    Q = U A F ΔTlm  ·  Q = ε Cmin (Th,in − Tc,in)  ·  NTU = UA / Cmin

    The LMTD method suits a design check when all four temperatures are known. The effectiveness-NTU method suits a rating problem: given the exchanger and the inlets, what comes out? The bench solves with ε-NTU and reports the LMTD and correction factor F it implies.

    Counterflow against parallel flow

    In counterflow the cold outlet can climb above the hot outlet: a temperature cross. In parallel flow both streams approach the same mixed temperature and the cold outlet can never exceed the hot outlet, so effectiveness is capped at 1/(1 + Cr). Same area, same flows, less duty.

    Capacity rates

    C = ṁ cp  ·  Cr = Cmin/Cmax

    The stream with the smaller capacity rate changes temperature the most and sets the maximum possible duty. Balanced flows (Cr = 1) give parallel temperature lines and the lowest effectiveness for a given NTU; a very unbalanced exchanger (Cr → 0, as with condensing steam) reaches ε = 1 − e−NTU whatever the arrangement.

    Fouling

    1/U = 1/Uclean + Rf

    Deposits add a resistance in series. It grows with time and with hot-side temperature, and its effect is largest on exchangers with a high clean U: a plate exchanger loses proportionally more to the same deposit than an oil cooler. Cleaning resets it; the traces show the duty recovering.

    Flow and U

    Film coefficients rise with velocity (about the 0.8 power in turbulent flow), so more flow raises U as well as the capacity rate, but pressure drop rises with the square of flow and pumping power with the cube. The bypass valve trades duty for a stable hot outlet.

    About the numbers

    [Illustrative data, for training only.] Constant properties per fluid, film coefficients scaled from the clean U at design flows, standard ε-NTU relations (counterflow, parallel, 1-2 shell-and-tube, cross-flow both unmixed), fouling growing with time on a compressed clock. No phase change, no property variation with temperature.

    How to use the trainer

    • Pick an arrangement, set both streams (fluid, flow, inlet temperature), the area and the clean U, then Start. Read the duty, effectiveness, NTU, outlet temperatures, LMTD and pressure drops.
    • Profiles show the two streams along the exchanger with the approach and pinch marked; the ε-NTU chart shows every arrangement at the present capacity ratio with your point on it.
    • Fouling builds on the fouling clock and the duty falls; clean the exchanger to recover it. The bypass valve lets you control the hot outlet.
    • Record readings to compare arrangements, flows and sizes in the tasks.

    Keys: Space start / stop · C clean · R record · 1–5 arrangement · Esc close
