    Offset and angularity

    y(x) = O + A·x/100  ·  shim at a foot = −y(xfoot)  ·  A = (yrear − yfront) / (B − A) × 100

    Misalignment at the coupling is an offset (the two centrelines parallel but apart) plus an angle (they cross). Both are corrected at the motor feet: the shaft is a straight line, so the shim change at each foot is simply the height of the motor centreline above the pump centreline at that foot, with the opposite sign. Two feet distances, two readings, two shims.

    Indicator methods

    rim TIR = 2 × offset  ·  face TIR = angle × face diameter / 100  ·  reverse dial: two rims across a span give offset and angle

    A rim indicator on one hub reads twice the offset as the shafts turn together; a face indicator reads the angle times the diameter it sweeps. Reverse-dial readings on both hubs give the offset at two planes, which is the same information. Lasers read offset and angle directly and do the arithmetic. Every method needs the bracket sag measured on a straight bar and subtracted, or the vertical readings are wrong by twice the sag.

    Thermal growth and soft foot

    Machines move as they warm: a motor typically rises a few hundredths of a millimetre at the shaft. Aligning to zero cold means misaligned hot, so the cold target is the negative of the growth. A soft foot (a foot that does not sit flat) distorts the frame every time its bolt is tightened, so the readings will not repeat; it must be found with an indicator at each foot and shimmed before alignment starts.

    What misalignment does

    A flexible coupling accommodates misalignment by flexing, and the reaction it develops loads the bearings and seals of both machines and shows up as vibration, especially at twice running speed. Bearing life falls with the cube of the load. Tolerances tighten with speed because the same offset makes larger forces and more cycles.

    How to use the bench

    • Read the rim and face dials (or the reverse-dial and laser readouts) in the vertical and horizontal planes. Mind the bracket sag.
    • Correct: work out the shims at the front and rear feet from the foot distances, then apply them with the sliders; switch to the plan view and jack the motor sideways. Show solution reveals the answer, Apply does it for you.
    • Before you start run the soft-foot check; if a foot shows a gap, shim it first. If the motor grows when hot, align to the hot target.
    • Start the set to see what the misalignment does: 1× and 2× vibration, coupling reaction, bearing temperature. Record readings for the tasks.
