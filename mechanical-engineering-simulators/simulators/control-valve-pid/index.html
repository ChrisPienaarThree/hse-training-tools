    Valve characteristics

    Q = Kv f(x) √Δpv  ·  linear f = x  ·  equal % f = R^{x−1}  ·  authority = Δpv,open / Δpsystem

    The inherent characteristic is what the valve does with a constant pressure drop. Installed in a pipe, the drop across the valve falls as it opens (the pipe takes more of the pump head), so every characteristic bends towards quick opening. With low authority a linear valve becomes almost on-off near closed; an equal-percentage valve compensates and stays roughly linear in flow, which is why it is the usual choice.

    PID

    OP = Kc [ e + (1/Ti) ∫e dt + Td de/dt ]

    Proportional action alone leaves an offset because it needs an error to hold the valve anywhere but its bias. Integral action keeps pushing until the error is zero, but too much of it (short Ti) makes the loop oscillate. Derivative anticipates, and is applied to the measurement so setpoint steps do not kick the valve. Anti-windup stops the integral accumulating while the valve is on a limit; without it the loop overshoots badly when the setpoint becomes reachable again.

    Identification and tuning

    FOPDT: K = ΔPV/ΔOP · τ = 1.5 (t₆₃ − t₂₈) · θ = t₆₃ − τ  ·  lambda: Kc = τ / K(λ + θ), Ti = τ  ·  ZN closed loop: Kc = 0.6 Ku, Ti = Pu/2, Td = Pu/8

    A manual step gives the process gain, time constant and dead time from the response. Lambda tuning sets a gentle closed loop; Ziegler–Nichols from the ultimate gain and period gives a faster, more oscillatory one. Dead time is the enemy: the more of it, the lower the gain you can use.

    Stiction

    A sticking valve does not move until the actuator force overcomes friction, then jumps. With integral action the controller keeps ramping until the valve jumps past the setpoint, and the loop limit-cycles with a square-ish PV and a triangular OP. A positioner with its own feedback largely hides it; the real cure is packing maintenance.

    About the numbers

    [Illustrative data, for training only.] The flow loop is a first-order lag plus dead time on the installed valve flow; the level tank integrates inflow minus outflow; the temperature loop is a slower lag with more dead time. Stiction is a simple deadband-and-slip model. Real loops add nonlinearity, valve hysteresis and transmitter dynamics.

    How to use the bench

    • Manual first: the controller starts in manual. Move the manual output and watch the valve stem, the flow and the PV respond. A step of 10 % and a minute of patience gives an identified process model and a suggested tuning.
    • Auto: switch the mode, set Kc, Ti and Td, and step the setpoint with the +10 / −10 buttons. The readouts give overshoot, settling time and IAE for the last step; oscillation is detected and its period shown.
    • Valve: change the characteristic, the system resistance (authority) and stiction, and watch the installed characteristic chart and the OP-against-stem trace.
    • Disturbances: move the pump pressure or the load and watch the loop recover. Record readings to compare tunings and valves in the tasks.
