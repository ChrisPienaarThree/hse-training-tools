Diesel overfill of tank T-101 during night transfer

RCA-EX-01 · Coastal Fuel Terminal (fictional) · Worked example
Date and time2026-09-18, 03:40LocationTank T-101, bund B-1InvestigatorLead investigatorTeamLead investigator; Operations superintendent; Instrument technician; HSE adviserStatusIn review

1. Description of the incident

During a night ship-to-tank transfer, diesel overflowed from tank T-101 into bund B-1. About 14 m³ was contained in the bund and recovered; no injuries and no off-site release.

2. Conclusion

The transfer was not stopped because the level gauge LT-101 froze at 78 % and the single night-shift operator, busy with a ship arrival, did not cross-check the level. The independent high-high trip LSHH-101 failed because its float was seized; proof testing had dropped out of the maintenance system. The independent alarm LAH-102 was probably missed in an alarm flood (still being confirmed).

Root causes

  • E-06 LSHH-101 level switch failed on demand (float stuck) (Maintenance) Confirmed
    Why: Proof testing of tank overfill switches dropped from the CMMS schedule; no tracking of overdue SIS tests.

Causal factors

  • E-04 LT-101 servo gauge displacer stuck (frozen reading) (Equipment / hardware) Confirmed
    Why: Gauge not in the planned maintenance system after the 2024 replacement.
  • E-03 Level cross-check not done during the transfer (Human performance) Confirmed
    Why: Transfer procedure has no hold point for cross-checking level near the end of a transfer.
  • E-08 Control-room operator did not respond to the independent high-level alarm LAH-102 (Human performance) Likely
    Why: Alarm flood during ship arrival; LAH-102 has the same priority as nuisance alarms.

Contributing factors

  • H-01 Single operator on night shift, also handling a ship arrival (Organisation / management) Confirmed
    Why: Night manning reduced from two to one in 2025 without a management-of-change review.

7. Lessons learned

  • Independent overfill protection is only independent if it is tested.
  • Manning changes need a management-of-change review that covers peak workload, such as a ship arrival during a transfer.
