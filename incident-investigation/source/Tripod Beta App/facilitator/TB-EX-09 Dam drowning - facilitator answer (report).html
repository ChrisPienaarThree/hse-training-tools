1. Summary

Four trios describe the event: the catchment flood overflows the dam and floods the pumps; the decision to recover the pumps puts an old boat with two inexperienced contractor workers on the overflowing dam; the boat drifts towards the spillway and the workers leave it; and, with no life-saving equipment, swimming ability or rescue capability, both drown. Six barriers failed and seven were missing or inadequate. The Underlying Causes sit in flood defences and dam procedures, competing goals in an emergency, a contractor management process so cumbersome that managers routinely bypass it, uncontrolled disused equipment, and the absence of any requirement, training or equipment for work on or near water.

4 trio(s) · 6 failed, 6 missing/inadequate and 0 effective barrier(s) · 14 underlying cause(s) · 26 recommended action(s).

2. Description of the incident

After heavy rain higher up in the catchment, the mine's north return-water dam rose quickly and began to overflow. The mine became aware of the rising level too late, and the pump installation at the dam was being flooded. The engineering manager asked the owner of a small contracting company working on site to help save the pumps. The contractor and two of his workers, none of whom had done work on water before, took an old boat that had been stored on site unused for many years to reach the flooded equipment, with one person on the bank holding a line. The current and wind pushed the boat away faster than the shore attendant could hold it, and it drifted towards the high overflow. Afraid of being carried over the wall, the two workers left the boat to reach the bank. Neither could swim; they underestimated the distance to the dam side and their work clothes and boots hampered them. Both drowned. There was no pre-task risk assessment or job plan, no life jackets or rescue equipment at the dam, and the workers had no training for work on or near water, water rescue or first aid for drowning.

10. Lessons learned

  • In a flood emergency, stop work on or near the water; protect people before equipment.
  • A slow contractor process does not stop contractors being used: it pushes the work outside the process.
  • Work on or near water needs a plan, life jackets, a rescue capability and people who have been trained, every time.
  • Disused equipment left on site will be used: remove it or control it.
